Economic Indicators vs Stock Market Performance¶

Alex Thomas and Juan Cuadros Marino

Write-Up:

This project examines how two major economic indicators, the Federal Funds Effective Rate and the Consumer Price Index (CPI), relate to the performance of the NASDAQ Composite. Interest rates and inflation are two of the most closely watched forces in financial markets, and the NASDAQ is a natural index to study alongside them: it is heavily weighted toward technology and growth companies, whose valuations are often described as especially sensitive to changes in interest rates. We use 3 datasets, all accessed through FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data), a free database maintained by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. We communicate often over text about what we need to do for the project. We meet at least once a week, often twice, to work on the project together. We use a shared Google Colab notebook and a shared GitHub repository, so we both always work on the most up-to-date version.

Datasets:

  1. Federal Funds Effective Rate (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/fedfunds): The Federal Funds Effective Rate is the average interest rate banks charge each other for overnight loans, which the Federal Reserve steers by setting a target range. The data is monthly, reported as a percent, and covers July 1954 to September 2026.
  2. Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL): CPI is the main measure of inflation in the United States; it tracks how the price of a fixed basket of goods and services changes over time. The data is monthly, seasonally adjusted, and reported as an index where the 1982–84 average equals 100. It covers January 1947 to August 2026. Because CPI is an index level rather than a rate, we will convert it to a year-over-year percent change to get the inflation rate.
  3. NASDAQ Composite (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM): The NASDAQ Composite is a tech-heavy index of the stocks listed on the Nasdaq exchange. The data is reported as daily closing values and covers February 1971 to October 2026.

Questions:

  1. Does the NASDAQ perform worse in months when the Federal Reserve is raising interest rates than in months when it is cutting them?
  2. Do NASDAQ returns tend to be lower during periods of high inflation?
  3. Has the NASDAQ been more volatile during periods of high interest rates and high inflation than during calmer economic periods?

The NASDAQ dataset originally contains daily observations, while the CPI and Federal Funds Rate datasets contain monthly observations. To make the datasets compatible, we converted NASDAQ to monthly data by keeping the closing value from the last available trading day of each month. After this transformation, the NASDAQ dataset contains 320 monthly observations from January 2000 through August 2026.

Examples
--------

    The simplest way to use nbconvert is

            > jupyter nbconvert mynotebook.ipynb --to html

            Options include ['asciidoc', 'custom', 'html', 'latex', 'markdown', 'notebook', 'pdf', 'python', 'qtpdf', 'qtpng', 'rst', 'script', 'slides', 'webpdf'].

            > jupyter nbconvert --to latex mynotebook.ipynb

            Both HTML and LaTeX support multiple output templates. LaTeX includes
            'base', 'article' and 'report'.  HTML includes 'basic', 'lab' and
            'classic'. You can specify the flavor of the format used.

            > jupyter nbconvert --to html --template lab mynotebook.ipynb

            You can also pipe the output to stdout, rather than a file

            > jupyter nbconvert mynotebook.ipynb --stdout

            PDF is generated via latex

            > jupyter nbconvert mynotebook.ipynb --to pdf

            You can get (and serve) a Reveal.js-powered slideshow

            > jupyter nbconvert myslides.ipynb --to slides --post serve

            Multiple notebooks can be given at the command line in a couple of
            different ways:

            > jupyter nbconvert notebook*.ipynb
            > jupyter nbconvert notebook1.ipynb notebook2.ipynb

            or you can specify the notebooks list in a config file, containing::

                c.NbConvertApp.notebooks = ["my_notebook.ipynb"]

            > jupyter nbconvert --config mycfg.py
