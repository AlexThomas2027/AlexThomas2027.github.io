Write-Up:¶

This project examines how three major economic indicators, the Consumer Sentiment index, the Federal Funds Effective Rate and the Consumer Price Index (CPI), relate to the performance of the NASDAQ Composite. Interest rates and inflation are two of the most closely watched forces in financial markets, and the NASDAQ is a natural index to study alongside them: it is heavily weighted toward technology and growth companies, whose valuations are often described as especially sensitive to changes in interest rates. We use 4 datasets, all accessed through FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data), a free database maintained by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.

Datasets:¶

  1. Federal Funds Effective Rate (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/fedfunds): The Federal Funds Effective Rate is the average interest rate banks charge each other for overnight loans, which the Federal Reserve steers by setting a target range. The data is monthly, reported as a percent, and covers July 1954 to September 2026.
  2. Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL): CPI is the main measure of inflation in the United States; it tracks how the price of a fixed basket of goods and services changes over time. The data is monthly, seasonally adjusted, and reported as an index where the 1982–84 average equals 100. It covers January 1947 to August 2026. Because CPI is an index level rather than a rate, we will convert it to a year-over-year percent change to get the inflation rate.
  3. NASDAQ Composite (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM): The NASDAQ Composite is a tech-heavy index of the stocks listed on the Nasdaq exchange. The data is reported as daily closing values and covers February 1971 to October 2026.
  4. Consumer Sentiment (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UMCSENT): University of Michigan's Consumer Sentiment index measures how U.S. households feel about their finances and the overall economy, based on a monthly survey. The data is monthly and reported as an index where the first quarter of 1966 equals 100.

Questions:¶

  1. Does the NASDAQ perform worse in months when the Federal Reserve is raising interest rates than in months when it is cutting them?
  2. Do NASDAQ returns tend to be lower during periods of high inflation?
  3. Has the NASDAQ been more volatile during periods of high interest rates and high inflation than during calmer economic periods?
  4. Do NASDAQ returns tend to be stronger when consumer sentiment is high or rising?

Creating Variables for the Analysis¶

In [ ]:
# Year-over-year inflation
cpi["inflation"] = cpi["CPIAUCSL"].pct_change(12, fill_method=None) * 100

# Monthly change in interest rates
fedfunds["rate_change"] = fedfunds["FEDFUNDS"].diff()

# Monthly change in consumer sentiment
sentiment["sentiment_change"] = sentiment["UMCSENT"].diff()

Inflation measures the percentage change in prices compared with the same month one year earlier. We also calculate monthly changes in interest rates and consumer sentiment because changes may be more useful than just their levels.

Model Questions¶

Model 1: Does high inflation lower NASDAQ returns?

  • Dependent variable: NASDAQ monthly return
  • Independent variable: inflation rate
  • Method: We will use a t-test to check whether the average NASDAQ return in the highest inflation group is significantly different from the lowest inflation group. We will also run a linear regression of NASDAQ returns on inflation.
  • Why: In EDA 4, average returns dropped from 1.40% in the low inflation group to −0.44% in the high inflation group, and inflation had the strongest correlation with returns in EDA 1.

Model 2: Can consumer sentiment predict whether the NASDAQ goes up or down?

  • Dependent variable: market direction
  • Independent variables: the previous month's change in consumer sentiment, inflation, and interest rate change
  • Method: We will use logistic regression, a model that predicts a yes-or-no outcome. We will train the model on earlier years and test it on later years to see how accurately it predicts.
  • Why: In EDA 7, the NASDAQ rose in 66.7% of months when sentiment improved, compared with 53.2% when it declined. We use the previous month's sentiment because our EDA compared values from the same month, which cannot show whether sentiment affects the market or the market affects sentiment.
